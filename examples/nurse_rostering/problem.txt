Nurse rostering, SchedulingPeriod-3.0 XML format. Assign each employee one shift
per day, or a day off, across the planning period, minimizing the weighted sum
of soft-constraint violations.

Three instances ship here, all from the public benchmark set at
https://www.schedulingbenchmarks.org/nrp/, and the published roster kept
alongside each one is PROVEN OPTIMAL -- that site marks proven optima in bold in
its results table, and all three of these are bold:

  QMC-2.ros        19 employees x 28 days x 3 shifts (E, L, N)      optimum  29
  BCV-3.46.2.ros   46 employees x 26 days x 3 shifts (N, L, V)      optimum 894
  ERMGH.ros        41 employees x 42 days x 4 shifts (D, DH, E, N)  optimum 779

They are not three sizes of the same instance. They disagree about skills, about
what a <Cover> block contains, about which of the four request tags exist, about
which pattern symbols exist, and about what a weight of 1000 means. Every "the
format does X" sentence below is therefore scoped to the instance it was learned
from; the set exists so that no QMC-2 habit can silently become a format rule.
ERMGH is the newest arrival and it refuted two such habits on contact -- see its
section.

THE FORMAT HAS NO HARD CONSTRAINTS, in any of the three instances. Every rule in the file
is soft; hardness is encoded as weight magnitude, and the magnitudes are
instance-specific. The objective is the weighted sum of all violations, so the
objective IS the problem. Exactly one hard constraint exists and it is not
written in any of the files: an employee works at most one shift per day.

================================================================================
QMC-2.ros
================================================================================

Assign each of 19 employees one shift per day (Early, Late, Night) or a day off
across a 28-day period.

QMC-2.ros is a real hospital instance: the ophthalmology ward at Queen's Medical
Centre University Hospital NHS Trust, Nottingham, March 2001, from the public
benchmark set at https://www.schedulingbenchmarks.org/nrp/. The published lower
bound is 29 and a cost-29 solution is published, so 29 is optimal.
QMC-2.Solution.29.roster is that published solution, kept here as the scorer's
golden test rather than as something to copy from.

QMC-2's weight ladder: 1000 is de facto hard (shift succession, the
consecutive-day caps, the coverage band), 100 is the hour limits, 1 is a genuine
preference. This ladder is QMC-2's, not the format's -- BCV-3.46.2 uses entirely
different magnitudes, so nothing may key off "1000 means hard" in general.

Rule families, all per-employee except coverage:

- Pattern rules (121 <Match> blocks over 19 contracts). Each counts how often a
  set of short patterns occurs in that employee's 28-day string and charges
  max(0, matches - threshold) * weight. Hit counts of the several patterns in
  one block are SUMMED, not OR-ed. The eleven rules are: No N-E, No N-L, Max 6
  consecutive working days, Min 2 consecutive working days, No half weekends,
  Max 3 working weekends, Min 2 consecutive 'N' shifts, and four hour caps.
- Workload rules (57 <TimeUnits> blocks). Hours in tenths of an hour, summed
  over three overlapping two-week windows: days 0-13, 7-20 and 14-27, and only
  those three. Per-shift duration comes from <ShiftTypes><TimeUnits>
  (E=75, L=75, N=100), never from EndTime - StartTime, which is 15 minutes
  longer than the paid figure for every shift.
- Coverage (52 <Cover> blocks, 31 of them skill-qualified). Specified per day of
  the week and expanded over the 28 days. This is the ONLY rule family that sums
  across employees; delete it and the instance falls apart into 19 independent
  single-nurse problems.
- Requests: 223 day-off requests (168 at weight 1, 55 at weight 1000) and 155
  shift-on requests, all at weight 1.

================================================================================
BCV-3.46.2.ros
================================================================================

Assign each of 46 employees one shift per day (N, L, V) or a day off across a
26-day period, 2000-03-06 to 2000-03-31. V is a Vacation shift, and it is an
ordinary assignable shift type with its own duration -- not a second spelling of
a day off.

Provenance, and it is NOT QMC-2's. QMC-2 is one real ward's historical roster.
BCV-3.46.2 is not: the benchmark site lists its problem as ANROM (Advanced Nurse
Rostering Model), a rostering model abstracted from Belgian hospital practice in
the literature the site cites for it (references BRU07, BRU09, BUR07b, BUR07c),
rather than a transcript of one hospital's month. Describing it as "a real
hospital instance" would be borrowing QMC-2's provenance for a file that does
not have it.

BCV-3.46.2.Solution.894.roster is the published cost-894 solution, found in 2008
by F. Xue, C.Y. Chan and W.H. Ip and shown in bold -- proven optimal -- on the
site. It is kept here for the same reason QMC-2's is: as the scorer's golden
test, not as something to copy from.

BCV's weight vocabulary, which shares nothing with QMC-2's ladder:

  cover     10000  (PrefOverStaffing and PrefUnderStaffing, the only two defined)
  requests     50  (every one of the 768, whatever the tag)
  rules    1 .. 1000, mostly 1, 3 and 5, with 2 on the workload caps

Note what this rules out. QMC-2's "1000 is de facto hard, 100 is the hour
limits" reading is not portable: here 1000 appears on 5 <Match> blocks, the
workload caps sit at weight 2, and the genuinely near-hard rule family is cover,
three orders of magnitude above everything else.

Rule families:

- Pattern rules (122 <Match> blocks, but over only 5 contracts, so 46 employees
  produce 1124 per-employee match instances -- roughly nine times QMC-2's 121).
  30 of the 122 carry no <Label> element at all and parse to the empty label;
  that is this file's structure, not a parse failure. One label can carry
  several thresholds: the 44 `Max shift types per week` blocks cap between 1
  and 5.
- Workload rules (5 <TimeUnits> blocks, one per contract, over the whole
  period). The units are this file's own -- N=10, L=8, V=7 -- and are NOT
  QMC-2's tenths of an hour. Nothing may convert between them.
- Coverage (23 <Cover> blocks -- 21 under <DayOfWeekCover>, 2 under
  <DateSpecificCover> -- none skill-qualified, all carrying only <Preferred>
  with no Min/Max band). There is no <Skills> section in this file at all, so
  the skill branch of cover never fires. As in QMC-2 this is the only rule
  family that sums across employees.
- Requests: 768 in total, all at weight 50 -- 138 DayOff, 132 DayOn, 498
  ShiftOff, and no ShiftOn whatsoever. QMC-2 uses the DayOff/ShiftOn diagonal of
  the request 2x2; BCV uses the other three corners. Between them all four are
  exercised by a real instance.

Format subtleties that decide whether a model is right:

- <RegionStart>/<RegionEnd> slice the SEARCH TEXT, not a validity period, so the
  ENTIRE match window must fit inside the region -- not merely its first day.
- <Start>N</Start> pins a match to absolute day N; <StartDay>Saturday</StartDay>
  allows any Saturday. Anchor and region stack: both filters apply.
- The max(0, .) in the penalty formula is in BOTH the Max and Min forms and is
  unrelated to which tag was used. It only denies credit for over-satisfying.
- <NotShift>N</NotShift> matches anything except N, a day off included.
- <ShiftGroup>All</ShiftGroup> resolves against <ShiftGroups>; `All` is an
  instance-defined ID meaning {E, L, N} here, not a keyword.
- Employee P uses contract O, and contract P is unused. Follow <ContractID>.
- The Preferred coverage penalty applies inside the [Min, Max] band too.
- A nurse holding both skills satisfies both skill minima with one assignment.
- <NotGroup>ON</NotGroup> is <NotShift> one level up: anything OUTSIDE the group,
  a day off included. BCV spells "free day" this way rather than as
  <Shift>-</Shift>, so reading it as "some working shift outside the group"
  makes every free-weekend rule unfalsifiable.
- <DateSpecificCover> names one absolute <Date> and REPLACES the
  <DayOfWeekCover> block for that (day, shift). It does not stack with it, and
  it does not repeat weekly. This one is not documented anywhere; the published
  optimum settles it. BCV's day 7 is a Monday whose weekday block prefers 6 on
  N while its date-specific block prefers 4, and the optimum staffs 4; day 13 is
  a Sunday, 4 against 6, and the optimum staffs 6. At 10000 per head a single
  unit of deviation costs more than the entire optimum, so the roster can only
  be optimal if exactly one block binds. Scoring both scores that same roster
  40894. The override is keyed per (day, shift), not per day: day 7 carries a
  date-specific block for N only, and its L and V still follow the Monday block.
- BCV's employee IDs are numeric strings listed OUT of numeric order -- the file
  opens "1", "46", "2". Anything that sorts them as text reorders the roster
  relative to the instance.
- <MaxConsecutiveFreeDays> appears once in BCV-3.46.2.ros and only inside an XML
  comment. It is dead and is deliberately not implemented.

================================================================================
ERMGH.ros
================================================================================

Assign each of 41 employees one shift per day (D, DH, E, N) or a day off across
a 42-day period, 2002-06-02 to 2002-07-13. Six weeks, not four: nothing may
assume a 28-day horizon. Every shift pays 8 <TimeUnits>, so a workload limit
here is a pure shift COUNT -- `Min 80` over a fortnight means ten shifts.

The benchmark site lists ERMGH under Emergency Room / general hospital data. Its
published cost-779 roster is shown in bold -- proven optimal -- and is kept here
as the scorer's golden test, the same role QMC-2's and BCV's play.

ERMGH's weights, a third vocabulary again:

  cover     100  (MinUnderStaffing and MaxOverStaffing)
                 1  (PrefUnderStaffing and PrefOverStaffing)
  requests  10 on 1243 of them, 1000 on 271
  rules     100 on 1030 blocks, 1000 on 36

Rule families:

- Pattern rules (843 <Match> blocks over 41 contracts -- one contract per
  employee, so 843 per-employee instances and not BCV's multiplication). 18 of
  them carry a <Min> sense; no other instance has one. NOT ONE carries a
  <Label>, so every rule penalty lands in the empty-string bucket.
- Workload rules (227 <TimeUnits> blocks, 104 of them <Min>). Three CONSECUTIVE,
  non-overlapping fortnights -- days 0-13, 14-27, 28-41 -- unlike QMC-2's three
  windows sliding a week at a time.
- Coverage (98 <Cover> blocks, 14 on each weekday, EVERY ONE skill-qualified and
  EVERY ONE stated as a <TimePeriod> rather than a <Shift>). No
  <DateSpecificCover> at all.
- Requests: 1514 -- 1 DayOff, 605 ShiftOn, 908 ShiftOff, and an empty
  <DayOnRequests> element. The published optimum satisfies every single one of
  them, and every rule too: its whole cost of 779 is coverage.

What ERMGH refutes, and each was a live assumption in this example's code
before it arrived:

- A <Cover> block does NOT have to name a <Shift>. ERMGH gives a <TimePeriod>
  instead -- a clock interval such as 15:30-19:30 -- and the headcount it speaks
  about is everyone whose shift is on duty for the WHOLE of it. Containment, not
  overlap: 15:30-19:30 is staffed by DH (12:00-20:00) and E (15:30-23:30);
  12:00-15:30 is staffed by D and DH, and not by E, which only starts as it
  ends. One period wraps midnight (23:30-07:30, shift N) and so does N itself,
  so the resolution is an arc comparison, not `start <= end`. parse_instance.py
  resolves the interval to that shift set once and hands every consumer a
  `shifts` list, which is `[shift]` for the other two instances.
- A skill-qualified block is NOT a bare minimum. QMC-2's skill blocks all carry
  only a <Min>, which reads like a rule about the format; ERMGH's skill-1 blocks
  carry <Max> and <Preferred> and no <Min> at all. The Min/Max/Preferred
  vocabulary is the same on both kinds of block -- the skill only changes WHO is
  counted. Ignoring Max/Preferred on a skill block scores ERMGH's published
  optimum at 0 rather than 779, and that failure is silent, because a roster
  that violates nothing else really does total 0 under that reading.
- <Shift>$</Shift> is a WILDCARD, not a shift ID: any working shift, a day off
  excluded. ERMGH writes 1288 of them and neither other instance has one. Its
  meaning is settled by the idiom it always appears in -- three patterns `-$`,
  `$-`, `$$` summed under one `Max 1` limit, which is "at most one working
  weekend". Read as "any working shift" those three score exactly one hit per
  worked weekend and zero for a free one. Read as "anything at all" a FREE
  weekend matches all three and scores 3, so the rule would fire hardest on the
  rosters it exists to reward; that reading scores the published optimum 226079.
  Read as a shift type literally named "$" it matches nothing, every rule scores
  0, and the published optimum STILL totals 779 -- a silent pass, which is why
  verify_parse.py pins the symbol census rather than trusting the total.
- A Shift(On|Off) request may name an inline <ShiftGroup> instead of a
  <ShiftTypeID>. ERMGH has four, each listing <Shift>E</Shift><Shift>D</Shift>
  as an anonymous pair. It is NOT a reference into the top-level <ShiftGroups>
  table -- that table does define `EorD` with the same members, but these blocks
  spell the shifts out -- so a parser that resolves it by name looks up a group
  ID that is not there. Any member satisfies the request.
- A <ShiftGroup> ID is not a shift ID. ERMGH's group `D` holds DH alone, and its
  group `E` holds E. Resolving a group by assuming the two namespaces coincide
  reads `D` as {D} and gets the wrong letter set.
- model.py's --harden is QMC-2-only and this instance shows why again. It zeroes
  every penalty at weight >= 100; ERMGH's optimum is 779 of cover penalty at
  weight 1 sitting alongside rules at weight 100, so hardening cuts off its own
  optimum. model_regular.py and model_bounds.py replace it with --upper-bound N,
  which reads only the instance's own weights and carries across all three.

================================================================================

All four request tags parse into one `Request` record carrying `wants` and an
optional `shifts` list, because the tags differ only in those two flags:

    DayOff   (wants=False, shifts=None)  ShiftOff (wants=False, shifts=["V"])
    DayOn    (wants=True,  shifts=None)  ShiftOn  (wants=True,  shifts=["E"])

`shifts` is a LIST rather than a single ID only because of ERMGH's four inline
<ShiftGroup> requests; for every other request in every instance it holds one
element and the membership test degenerates to the equality it used to be.

model.py and scorer.py each build that 2x2 independently -- one out of CP-SAT
literals, one in plain Python -- and neither imports the other's version.

Files:

  instance.py         The 9 Pydantic records -- Instance, Contract, Match,
                      Limit, Pattern, WorkloadLimit, Employee, CoverBlock,
                      Request -- plus load_instance(json_path). Imports no
                      XML; every runtime script reads an instance through
                      this file, never parse_instance.py.
  roster.py           Roster = dict[str, list[str]]; load_roster(path)
                      loads it. No record class: there is no invariant
                      beyond "dict of lists of strings" for one to enforce.
  parse_instance.py   XML -> the instance.py records, written out as
                      compact JSON. Discriminates the two meanings of
                      <TimeUnits> by parent node, both kinds of cover
                      block, and both ways a cover block names its
                      headcount (<Shift> and <TimePeriod>).
  parse_roster.py     .roster XML -> roster JSON. Houses read_roster_xml,
                      moved out of scorer.py so scorer.py, checker.py,
                      verify_model.py and every model's --fix-roster take
                      roster input from JSON only, never XML.
  verify_parse.py     Gate: asserts each parse reproduces every published count.
                      QMC-2 (19 employees, 121 <Match>, 235 <Pattern>, 52
                      <Cover>, 223+155 requests, the <Weight> histogram),
                      BCV-3.46.2 (46 employees, 122 <Match>, 155 <Pattern>, 23
                      <Cover> of which 2 date-specific, 768 requests, the symbol
                      census that pins <NotGroup>), and ERMGH (41 employees, 843
                      <Match>, 1345 <Pattern>, 98 <Cover> all skill-qualified
                      and all time-period, 1514 requests, the period-to-shift
                      resolution re-derived by hand, and the symbol census that
                      pins `$`). 60 + 102 + 36 = 198 checks.
  model.py            The CP-SAT model, for any instance via --instance. All
                      rules soft at true weights by default; --harden is an
                      optional second-pass speedup whose soundness argument is
                      QMC-2-specific, and --fix-roster pins the variables to a
                      given roster.
  model_regular.py    Same objective, pattern rules routed through a DFA where
                      the shape allows. Its DFA reads each symbol as a LETTER
                      SET rather than a literal, so pinning it to a published
                      optimum re-derives the symbol semantics independently of
                      model.py -- including `$`.
  model_bounds.py     Same window encoding as model.py plus --upper-bound,
                      --prove and the redundant aggregate cuts.
  shift_literals.py   The pattern-symbol vocabulary as CP-SAT literals, shared
                      by the three model files, which had three byte-identical
                      copies of it. scorer.py keeps its OWN reading in plain
                      Python, and model_regular.py keeps its own letter-set
                      reading for the DFA; both restatements are deliberate and
                      survive.
  scorer.py           The independent scorer. Recomputes every penalty from the
                      instance and a finished roster, and shares no penalty code
                      with model.py -- that independence is what makes agreement
                      between the two mean anything. Takes the instance as an
                      optional second argument, defaulting to QMC-2.json.
  checker.py          scorer.py wrapped in the MCP checker protocol: rejects
                      unless the model's objective equals the recomputed total.
                      Needs no per-instance code -- it grades whatever `problem`
                      names, and inherits new rule support from the scorer.
  verify_model.py     The acceptance criterion, executable. Three gates per
                      instance: scorer vs. the published roster, model pinned to
                      that same roster, and model-vs-scorer agreement across
                      several seeds. 20 gates.
  solution.csv        A 19 x 28 QMC-2 roster (rows A..S) kept as a sample of
                      the employees-x-days CSV shape scorer.py reads. The models
                      no longer WRITE this shape -- they emit the roster on
                      stdout with everything else -- so these two are static
                      files, not the output of the last run.
  solution_bcv.csv    The same, 46 x 26, for BCV-3.46.2.
  *.json              14 committed artifacts: <name>.json for each of the 7
                      instances (parse_instance.py) and
                      <name>.Solution.<n>.json for each of the 7 published
                      rosters (parse_roster.py). Regenerating either must
                      reproduce the committed bytes -- gated in
                      tests/examples/test_nurse_rostering.py -- so these
                      cannot silently drift from the converters that
                      produced them.

Reproduce:

  uv run examples/nurse_rostering/verify_parse.py
  uv run examples/nurse_rostering/verify_model.py
  uv run examples/nurse_rostering/model.py --time-limit 300
  uv run examples/nurse_rostering/model.py --instance BCV-3.46.2.json --time-limit 300
  uv run examples/nurse_rostering/model.py --instance ERMGH.json --time-limit 300
  uv run examples/nurse_rostering/scorer.py solution.csv          # a CSV roster
  uv run examples/nurse_rostering/scorer.py QMC-2.Solution.29.json
  uv run examples/nurse_rostering/scorer.py solution_bcv.csv BCV-3.46.2.json
  uv run examples/nurse_rostering/scorer.py BCV-3.46.2.Solution.894.json BCV-3.46.2.json
  uv run examples/nurse_rostering/scorer.py ERMGH.Solution.779.json ERMGH.json

================================================================================
Four more instances, for the optional-element fallbacks
================================================================================

The benchmark set has 51 instances in two dialects. The 28 "Date dialect" files
-- the ones whose requests carry an ISO <Date> -- are the ones this example
targets, and they are exactly the 28 with a published roster, so every one of
them arrives with its own golden test. The other 23 (Instance1..Instance24) are
a genuinely different dialect -- day offsets instead of dates, <Duration>
instead of <TimeUnits>, per-block cover weights written as attributes, and
employees carrying several <ContractID> elements -- and are NOT supported.

Of those 28, the parser reads 20 well enough to reproduce the published optimum
exactly. Most of the gap to the original three was not new semantics at all but
OPTIONAL ELEMENTS the first three instances all happened to include. Four small
files ship here to keep each fallback honest, chosen as the smallest instance
exercising one and having a published optimum to grade against:

  BCDT-Sep.ros    39 KB   optimum  100   no <CoverWeights>; <Match> with no <Weight>
  GPost.ros       18 KB   optimum    5   <Shift> with no <TimeUnits>
  Millar-2Shift-DATA1.ros  6 KB  optimum 0   <Employee> with no <Name>
  QMC-1.ros      139 KB   optimum   13   <ShiftGroupID> in a request

The fallbacks, and why each is a fallback and not a rewrite:

- <TimeUnits> absent -> the shift's CLOCK SPAN in minutes. Twenty-two instances
  omit the tag. It fires only where the file is silent: QMC-2 states 75 for a
  shift whose clock span is 90, so deriving a duration the file already gives
  turns a roster sitting exactly on its 750 boundary into a phantom violation.
  The fallback also changes the UNIT, to minutes; nothing may compare a duration
  across instances, which was already true.
- <Weight> absent on a <Max>/<Min> -> 1.
- <CoverWeights> absent -> 10 for all four keys. BCDT-Sep is the only instance
  that omits the element and its published cost-100 optimum pins the value: its
  rules account for exactly 80 and its cover for two units of Min understaffing,
  so the weight can only be 10. That is ONE data point, not a documented format
  rule, which is why verify_parse.py gates it. A declared <CoverWeights> is never
  topped up key by key -- BCV-3.46.2 names only the two Pref keys and that
  absence is itself an asserted fact.
- <Name> absent -> the employee ID. Display only; it never feeds a rule.
- <ShiftGroupID>L</ShiftGroupID> in a request is a REFERENCE resolved through
  <ShiftGroups>. It is NOT ERMGH's inline <ShiftGroup>, which spells its members
  out and is read in place. Three spellings now name a request's shifts, and
  reading any as another looks up a name that is not there.
- <StartDate>1996-11-02</StartDate> is the <Start> anchor written as a date.
  Implemented, but NOT validated against any published optimum: the only
  instance using it is Ikegami-2Shift-DATA1, which does not yet reproduce.

Eight of the 28 still do not reproduce, and the causes are known, not mysterious:

  Ikegami x4   <Cover> qualified by a <SkillGroup> rather than a single <Skill>
               (180-450 blocks each), read as unqualified cover and so wildly
               overcharged. <SkillGroups> is the table that resolves them.
  HED01, HED01b   overcharged; cause not yet isolated.
  ORTEC01, ORTEC02   UNDERcharged, 270 scored as 60 -- the dangerous direction,
               since an under-charge is what a missing rule family looks like.

None of these is implemented, and none is claimed. <FixedAssignments>, which
seven of them carry, is NOT the cause: it pins cells for a solver and cannot
change the score of a finished roster.

================================================================================

ERMGH is the easiest of the three for the model despite being the largest:
model.py proves its optimum of 779 in well under 60 seconds on 8 workers, where
BCV-3.46.2 reaches 894 in that budget without proving it.
